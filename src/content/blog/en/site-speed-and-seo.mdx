Yes, site speed affects Google rankings, but as a secondary factor — Google measures it through three metrics known as Core Web Vitals (LCP, INP, CLS). Content quality remains the primary factor; speed acts as a tie-breaker between sites of similar quality.

## Does site speed really affect Google rankings?

In June 2021, Google's Page Experience update officially turned Core Web Vitals into a ranking signal. But it's important to understand this correctly: it's **not the primary** factor — it's an **additional** one.

Google itself has stated that useful, relevant content still comes first. Core Web Vitals mainly shows up in two situations:

- If a page is **very slow** (for example, LCP over 4 seconds) — that's a clear negative signal
- If two sites offer equally good content — the faster one gets a small edge

In other words, a slow site isn't automatically "punished," but a fast site gives you a real, if modest, advantage over competitors.

## What are Core Web Vitals — the 3 core metrics

Google uses three specific metrics to evaluate sites. They're calculated from real Chrome user data — not a lab test.

| Metric | What it measures | Good value |
|---|---|---|
| **LCP** (Largest Contentful Paint) | How long the largest element on the page (usually a banner image or headline) takes to fully render | Under 2.5 seconds |
| **INP** (Interaction to Next Paint) | How quickly a page responds after a user clicks something | Under 200 milliseconds |
| **CLS** (Cumulative Layout Shift) | How much page elements unexpectedly "jump" while loading | Under 0.1 |

Google evaluates each metric at the 75th percentile of users — meaning your page needs to perform well for most visitors, not just in the best-case scenario.

> **Note:** INP replaced the older FID (First Input Delay) metric in March 2024 and remains the main responsiveness metric in 2026.

## What real damage does a slow site cause?

Beyond rankings, a slow site is a direct customer-loss problem. According to various studies:

- A page loading in **1 second** has a bounce rate of around 7%
- A page loading in **5 seconds** can see that rate climb to around 38%
- Each additional second of delay lowers conversion rates by a few percentage points on average
- According to Google's own research, **53%** of mobile users abandon a page that takes longer than 3 seconds to load

These numbers show that site speed isn't just an SEO concern — it's a factor that directly affects leads and revenue.

## How to check your site's speed for free

There are two official Google tools, both free:

1. **PageSpeed Insights** ([pagespeed.web.dev](https://pagespeed.web.dev)) — enter your site's URL and get LCP, INP, and CLS values plus specific fix recommendations in 10-15 seconds. This is a one-time snapshot of your current state.
2. **The Core Web Vitals report in Google Search Console** — shows real user experience data over time, sorting pages into "Good," "Needs improvement," and "Poor" categories. We cover setting this up in detail in our [Google Search Console guide](/blog/how-to-use-google-search-console).

## The biggest factors for speeding up a site

Without getting too technical, here are the three things that make the biggest practical difference:

- **Image size** — uncompressed, oversized images are the most common cause of slowdowns
- **Unnecessary third-party scripts** — extra widgets, multiple tracking codes, unneeded chat-widget scripts all add weight to a page
- **Low-quality or misconfigured hosting** — a slow server response can undo every other optimization

Fixing these three usually delivers a noticeable speed improvement without deep technical work.

## Is speed the only SEO factor?

No. Site speed is one piece of SEO, not a decisive one. Keyword targeting, content quality, backlinks, and technical SEO (indexing, structure) remain the core factors. Speed provides an edge once everything else is already right — but a slow site can't fully hide good content, and a fast site can't rescue thin content.

## FAQ: Common questions about site speed and SEO

**1. Does site speed directly affect Google rankings?**
Yes, but as a secondary factor. Content quality and relevance remain the most important factor; speed acts as a tie-breaker between similarly ranked sites.

**2. What are Core Web Vitals?**
Three metrics from Google: LCP (load speed, under 2.5s), INP (response speed, under 200ms), and CLS (visual stability, under 0.1). All are measured from real user data.

**3. How can I check my site's speed for free?**
Enter your site's URL into Google PageSpeed Insights — you'll get results and recommendations in seconds. The Core Web Vitals report in Search Console shows data over time.

**4. How many customers does a slow site actually lose?**
Studies show bounce rates for 5-second loads can reach ~38%, and 53% of mobile users abandon a page after more than 3 seconds.

**5. What's the first thing to fix to speed up a site?**
Reducing image size usually has the biggest impact, followed by removing unnecessary third-party scripts and choosing quality hosting.

**6. Is mobile speed measured the same way as desktop?**
No, Google evaluates them separately, and under mobile-first indexing, treats your site's mobile version as the primary one.
