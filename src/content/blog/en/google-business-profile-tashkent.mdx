To show up in Tashkent for searches like "restaurant near me" or "plumber Yunusabad," you first need this profile set up correctly — with an accurate rating, address, and hours.

## What is Google Business Profile, and why does it matter?

Google Business Profile (formerly Google My Business) is the card that shows up when someone searches for your business on Google or on Google Maps: address, hours, phone number, reviews, photos, and the directions button all come from this profile.

That's practically important for any business with a physical address or service area:

- restaurants, cafes, shops — places customers travel to;
- plumbers, electricians, handymen — services that come to the customer;
- clinics, tutoring centers, salons — services with a physical location;
- companies that meet clients at an office (legal, financial, and so on).

Without a profile, a business simply doesn't show up for searches like "restaurant in Tashkent" or "[service] near me" — those results come directly from Google Maps data, not from a website's ordinary search ranking.

## What determines ranking on Google Maps?

Google explains local pack ranking through three official factors:

| Factor | What it means | How to improve it |
|---|---|---|
| **Relevance** | How well the profile matches the search query | An accurate primary category, a complete list of services, a clear description |
| **Distance** | The gap between the searcher and the business | Can't be directly controlled — getting the exact address and service area right still matters |
| **Prominence** | Review count/rating, links, mentions | Collecting new reviews regularly, quality links to the website, mentions from local sources |

Two of the three factors — relevance and prominence — are fully within your control. You can't change distance, but working on the other two can help close the gap with closer competitors.

## How do you set up a Google Business Profile?

1. Go to **google.com/business** and enter your business name.
2. Choose the **most specific primary category** available (for example, a narrow category like "Uzbek restaurant" rather than just "Restaurant").
3. Enter an address or service area — businesses customers travel to (a shop or office) list an exact address, while services that come to the customer set a service area instead.
4. Add a phone number and website address.
5. Complete **verification** — Google picks the method automatically: a real-time video recording, phone/SMS, email, or a mailed code. Video verification is usually the fastest, often taking just a few minutes.

An unverified profile either doesn't show up in Google Maps search at all or stays stuck with a weak ranking — so this step shouldn't get skipped.

## What does it take to fill out a profile completely?

The most important step after verification is not leaving the profile half-empty:

- **Name, address, phone (NAP)** — must match **word for word** across your website, social media, and any other directories. Inconsistency confuses Google and hurts ranking.
- **10+ photos** — the exterior, the interior, a sample of the product or service.
- **Services and attributes** filled in completely (for example, "delivery available," "online ordering," "free parking").
- **Hours** — accurate, and updated around holidays.
- **Description** — a short piece of text describing the core service and city in natural language.

The more complete this data is, the higher the profile scores on the "relevance" factor.

## Why do reviews matter so much?

Reviews are the most visible part of the "prominence" factor. Two things matter in practice:

1. **Rating and count** — an average rating above 4.5 with more than 10 reviews builds trust.
2. **Review velocity** — a profile that picks up a handful of new reviews every month usually outperforms one that got 50 reviews once and then went two years without a single new one. Steady, ongoing activity is a signal to Google that a profile is alive.

Responding to every review, especially negative ones, promptly and professionally is worth doing — it's a positive signal to both the customer and Google's algorithm.

## How can a Tashkent business get an edge in "near me" searches?

Even though the distance factor can't be changed, a Tashkent business can still focus on:

- setting the service area to match actual coverage rather than exaggerating it;
- naturally using the district or neighborhood name in the description and posts;
- building "prominence" through mentions in local sources (business directories, local media);
- keeping the address on Google Business Profile in the exact same format as the contact page on the website.

## Common mistakes

- **One profile for multiple locations** — each address needs its own separate profile; otherwise Google may treat it as a policy violation and suspend the profile entirely.
- **Address or phone number that doesn't match the website** — this undermines trust.
- **Forgetting about the profile after verification** — if photos, posts, and review responses stop getting updated, the "prominence" signal weakens.
- **Choosing the wrong or an overly broad category** — picking a precise, narrow category instead of a generic one like "Service" noticeably improves relevance.
