**Who is safer to hand your website to: an independent developer or an agency?** The lower-risk choice is a contractor who understands the task, can show their work and whose responsibility is clear both during the project and after it. That arrangement is possible with a freelancer and with an agency.

In this article “safety” means project risks: work abandoned halfway, disagreements, losing access to your site. The technical security of the site itself needs a separate review.

## The practical difference between a freelancer and an agency

A freelancer is a specialist who works independently. Talking to them directly and agreeing on changes in a small task can be convenient. But you need to check whether one person’s time and experience are enough for what the project needs.

At an agency the work can be split between different specialists: one does design, another development, another testing. Confirm that this is in the proposal and applies to your project specifically. Behind the image of a big team, what matters is who actually does the work.

The table below gives practical direction for the choice. It is not a final verdict on every contractor.

| Criterion | Clarify with a freelancer | Clarify with an agency |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Communication | When do they reply? | Who is responsible for ongoing contact? |
| Scope | Can they do the whole job alone? | Which specialists will be assigned? |
| Continuity | How does work continue if they are temporarily unavailable? | If someone leaves, who takes over the information? |
| Testing | Who tests the result, and how? | Is there a separate testing stage? |
| Handover | When are files and access handed over? | Is there a handover checklist and a responsible person? |
| Later support | How much time can they give? | What does support include and how fast is the response? |

## When might a freelancer be a good fit?

If the requirements are clear, the scope is limited and it matches one specialist’s experience, a freelancer is worth considering. For example, a page presenting one service or a portfolio site.

Before choosing, look at genuinely similar work. Open the finished site on your phone, test the contact button and ask which part of that project the specialist did. A nice screenshot does not show every side of the work.

As the business owner, also consider whether you can set aside time to clarify requirements and review interim results. When working with one specialist, some of those tasks may stay with you.

## When should you consider an agency?

If several areas have to work together in the project, a team approach can help. In an online store, for example, products, orders, payments, design and search readiness all depend on each other.

But do not settle for “we do everything”. Ask who prepares the design, who tests the order flow and who you will talk to after launch. If some work is passed to another contractor, confirm that the party responsible for the result does not change.

When judging an agency’s experience it helps to look at past work, how they solved the business problem and whether the communication suits you. [Invest Northern Ireland’s business guide](https://www.nibusinessinfo.co.uk/content/how-find-right-designer-or-design-agency) also recommends clarifying the contractor’s experience, approach and project goals in advance.

## Compare prices on the same scope of work

One quote may cover only assembling the pages, while another also includes placing the text, mobile adaptation, testing and launch. Comparing the bottom-line numbers of such quotes is misleading.

Send both contractors the same short brief and ask them to list separately:

- how many pages and languages will be prepared;
- who provides the text, photos and translation;
- where the order or contact form is connected;
- how many rounds of revisions are included;
- what testing and launch cover;
- whether the domain, hosting and paid services are charged separately;
- how later updates and support are priced.

For example, “a five-page website” is not enough of a description. If you need text in two languages, product uploads or data moved from an old site, write that down too. You can discuss your project’s tasks through KATOV’s [website development service](/en/sayt-yaratish) page.

## Define acceptance criteria up front

“The site should work well” is hard to verify. Turn it into results that can be seen and tested.

For a training centre’s site, for example, you could write: “A user on a phone picks a course and enters their name and phone number. A test request reaches the assigned administrator. The user sees that the request was received.”

This example is hypothetical. It shows how to make an agreement concrete. In your own project, choose the most important action: a call, a booking, an order or a request.

It also helps to define interim stages: a page plan, the design, a working test version and the final handover. At each stage it should be clear who gives feedback and when revisions are sent. Discuss in advance how delays in text or approvals on the client’s side will affect the deadline.

## Will you be able to manage the site after the project?

Handover does not end when the site goes live. Within the agreed scope, you should be able to update it and, if needed, pass it to another specialist.

Ask the contractor for a handover list: admin access, project files, service accounts, a backup and a short guide. Discuss setting up the domain and hosting accounts under your business’s control; the specialist can be given the access they need separately.

If a ready-made platform is used, there may be limits on moving it elsewhere. Ask “Can I move the site later, what moves with it and which subscriptions continue?” before work starts.

## How does later support work?

Instead of “we’ll help”, discuss a specific situation: if the site stops sending requests, who do you contact, when will the message be seen and what is the process for fixing it?

Response time and time to fully resolve a problem are not the same. Fixing a bug in an agreed task and adding a new page are also different jobs. Make sure it is written down which of them the price includes.

[Business Victoria’s guide](https://business.vic.gov.au/business-information/ecommerce/build-a-website/know-what-to-ask-your-web-developer) treats a website as something that needs regular attention and recommends clarifying the terms of working with a developer through questions from the start.

## Ready-made questions for a contractor

You can send these questions to a freelancer and to an agency alike:

1. Can you show a project similar to ours and your role in it?
2. How did you understand the main action a user should take on our site?
3. What does your quote include, and what is charged separately?
4. Who will I talk to during the work, and when will I see interim results?
5. How do you test the mobile version, request forms and other key features?
6. How does the project continue if the main specialist is unavailable?
7. Which files, accounts and guides will I receive at the end?
8. How long does post-launch support last and what does it include?

Compare the answers in writing. Avoiding clarifying vague points, not showing a working example or leaving the deliverable undefined are reasons to look closer. These criteria apply regardless of the contractor’s size.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is it safe to hand a website to a freelancer?

It can be. Check similar projects, a clear work plan, the handover process and later support. It also matters how work continues if the specialist is temporarily unavailable.

### Does an agency guarantee quality?

The word “agency” alone is not enough. It should be clear who works on the project, who does the testing and who is responsible for the final result.

### How should I compare the quoted prices?

Level out the scope of pages, languages, text and images, integrations, testing, launch and later support. Account for separate costs too.

### What should I receive when the site is handed over?

Check the agreed project files, accounts under your business’s control, admin access, a backup and an update guide. Clarify platform-specific limits in advance.
